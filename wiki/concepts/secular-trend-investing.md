# Secular Trend Investing

## Definition

An investment orientation that anchors position-holding to multi-year or multi-decade structural shifts — "secular trends" — rather than to short-term market cycles, news events, or quarterly earnings surprises. The practical discipline is filtering: most daily market noise is irrelevant to thesis validity; positions are held or adjusted based on whether the underlying structural demand is intact, not whether the stock moved this week.

## Detail

The secular/cyclical distinction matters because it defines what counts as signal vs. noise. A cyclical investor reacts to interest rate moves, earnings beats, and macro headlines. A secular investor asks whether the structural driver of demand — in Nash's case, the AI infrastructure build-out — is still intact. Short-term price volatility is expected and discounted; it is not evidence against the thesis unless the structural driver itself has changed.

This orientation pairs naturally with [[wiki/concepts/picks-and-shovels-investing]]: once you identify companies with structural roles in a secular trend, the discipline of tuning out noise determines whether you actually capture the multi-year return. Selling during a correction because of short-term market news defeats the purpose of the framework.

The systematic investing corollary is important: Nash stresses that outcomes come from building a disciplined research and portfolio-construction process — not from reacting to tips or chasing momentum. The process (deep research → conviction → position sizing → holding) is the compound advantage, not any individual stock selection.

## Evidence & Examples

- Nash explicitly argues that AI mega trend investors should "ignore short-term market noise and news cycles in favor of holding quality companies tied to secular trends" [[wiki/sources/tom-nash-ai-infrastructure-investing]]
- The AI infrastructure build-out (data centers, semiconductors, power, networking) is the secular trend Nash identifies as a multi-year structural driver [[wiki/sources/tom-nash-ai-infrastructure-investing]]
