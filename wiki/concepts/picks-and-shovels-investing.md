# Picks and Shovels Investing

## Definition

An investment strategy that targets the suppliers of tools, infrastructure, and enabling services required by a major industry wave — rather than the speculative companies trying to win that wave directly. Derived from the Gold Rush observation that durable fortunes went to the picks-and-shovels sellers, not the prospectors.

## Detail

The core logic is risk reduction through structural demand: infrastructure companies earn revenue from the growth of an industry as a whole. They do not require a correct prediction of which applications or products ultimately win. In the AI context, companies at the compute, networking, power, data, observability, and security layers all benefit from rising AI adoption regardless of whether the winning AI applications are in healthcare, finance, robotics, or entertainment.

Tom Nash applies this framework to AI by identifying eight structural layers, each with leading public companies positioned as infrastructure suppliers:

1. **Physical Infrastructure** — cloud data centers (Google, Amazon AWS, Microsoft Azure, Oracle)
2. **Compute** — GPUs and semiconductors (Nvidia, AMD, Micron, ASML, TSMC)
3. **Networking** — (Arista Networks)
4. **Power and Cooling** — (Constellation Energy, Bloom Energy, Vertiv)
5. **Control / OS** — the platform layer that orchestrates AI workflows (Palantir)
6. **Data** — (MongoDB, Oracle)
7. **Observability** — monitoring AI systems in production (Datadog)
8. **Security** — (Crowdstrike, Zscaler)

The eight-layer map is a snapshot; the strategically durable point is the *framework*, not any particular company list.
